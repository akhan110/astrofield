Field Guide: Step-by-Step Workflow

A quick 4-step sequence for preparing and executing a successful night of imaging.

1

Set Location & Observe Dark Windows

Tap Location & GPS in the sidebar. Tap "Auto-Detect GPS" or manually set your coordinates. Check Sun & Twilight to see the exact minute true astronomical darkness begins (Sun ≤ −18°).

2

Add Your Telescope & Camera Profile

Open Equipment and tap the "+" button. Enter your sensor width, sensor height, pixel pitch (μm), and telescope focal length (mm). AstroField automatically calculates your optical resolution (''/px) and field-of-view.

3

Pick Top Scoring Targets & Frame

Browse the Catalog or check the Home screen for targets scoring above 80. Tap Framing Simulator to rotate your camera sensor angle (0°–360°) and compose your shot.

4

Polar Align & Queue Exposures

Level your equatorial mount and open Polar Alignment Clock. Position Polaris at the exact clock position shown on the dial. Then open Session Scheduler to track sub-exposures and meridian flips.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does AstroField require internet or cell service in the field?
No! The core philosophy of AstroField is 100% offline capability. Celestial mechanics, planet positions, deep-sky catalogs, and optical formulas run entirely on your phone’s CPU with zero internet connection.
Is the astronomical data real or simulated?
It is completely real. Target positions come from the verified OpenNGC and SIMBAD astronomical catalogs. Ephemeris and coordinate conversions use high-precision spherical trigonometry from the Astronomy Engine.
How does weather forecasting work offline?
Before traveling to your observation site, open the Weather screen to download the 7-day hourly forecast from Open-Meteo. AstroField caches this data so you can review cloud cover and seeing forecasts even without a signal.
Can I use AstroField in the Southern Hemisphere?
Yes. AstroField detects negative latitudes and automatically adapts the Polar Clock to display the Sigma Octantis reticle instead of Polaris.
