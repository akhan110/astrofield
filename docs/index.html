1. Overview & Offline Philosophy 100% Offline

AstroField is purpose-built for the realities of field astrophotography. Because prime dark-sky locations rarely offer cellular coverage, every calculation in AstroField runs on-device with zero internet reliance.

Local Celestial Mechanics

Using GeoEngine’s port of the Astronomy Engine library, AstroField performs real-time orbital and spherical trigonometry calculations directly on your phone’s CPU. Twilight transitions, planet positions, and target altitudes are computed dynamically for your exact GPS coordinate and elevation.

  • Calculates Sun & Moon rise, set, and astronomical darkness (Sun ≤ −18°).
  • Applies standard atmospheric refraction to all horizon calculations.
  • Protects night-adapted rhodopsin vision with dedicated red screen filters.

2. Quick-Start Field Workflow

Follow these 4 core steps before and during an imaging session:

  1. Set Observing Site: Tap Location & GPS and use GPS auto-fill or enter coordinates manually.
  2. Configure Equipment: Enter sensor dimensions (width/height), pixel pitch (μm), and telescope focal length (mm).
  3. Review Dark Windows: Open Sun & Twilight to inspect the astronomical darkness interval and moonless window.
  4. Queue & Aim: Select prime targets (> 80 score) in Catalog or Session Scheduler, verify framing in Framing Simulator, and align your mount with Polar Clock.

3. Framing Simulator Optics

The Framing Simulator calculates your camera sensor’s exact rectangular field of view and overlays it onto real deep-sky astrophotography imagery.

How to Use

  • Target Selection: Tap "Change" to pick from 207 Messier, NGC, IC, and Solar System objects with real DSS2 image cutouts.
  • Sensor Rotation: Slide the Camera Angle slider (0° to 360°) to preview how rotating your camera rotator or T-ring will frame the object.
  • Cardinal Markers: N, S, E, and W markers follow astrophotographical sky convention (East is left).

Image Scale & Sampling Formula

Resolution (''/px) = 206.265 × Pixel Pitch (μm) / Focal Length (mm)
  • Undersampled (> 2.0 ''/px): Sharp stars across wide fields.
  • Optimal (0.67 – 2.0 ''/px): Ideal balance between camera resolution and typical atmospheric seeing.
  • Oversampled (< 0.67 ''/px): Seeing-limited resolution.

4. Polar Alignment Clock

Equatorial mounts track the night sky smoothly only when aligned with the Earth's True Celestial Pole. Because Polaris is offset by ∼0.65°, it must be placed at a specific reticle clock angle.

Polar Scope Alignment Steps

  • Level your tripod using a bubble level.
  • Orient your mount's optical polar scope so that the 12 o'clock reticle marker points straight up.
  • Look at AstroField's Polar Clock dial: adjust mount altitude/azimuth bolts until Polaris matches the highlighted marker position.
  • Southern Hemisphere: Automatically displays the Sigma Octantis constellation reticle.

5. Session Scheduler & Timeline

Plan and sequence an entire night’s multi-target exposure queue:

  • Queue multiple targets with specific sub-exposure counts (e.g. 40 frames) and durations (e.g. 180s).
  • Visualizes astronomical darkness in dark purple and Moon rise/set intervals.
  • Highlights meridian crossing times when German Equatorial Mounts require a meridian flip.

6. Point-to-Sky AR Compass

Aim your telescope mount or binoculars with real-time sensor feedback:

  • Calibration: Move your device in a slow figure-8 motion for 5 seconds away from metal tripod legs.
  • Target Lock: Tap any target on the AR radar to activate live tilt/pan guidance arrows.

7. Red Light & Field Optics Tools

NPF Exposure Rule (Untracked Star Trailing)

Calculates maximum exposure time before stars begin to trail:

t_max = (16.9 × N + 0.1 × f + 13.7 × p) / (f × cos(δ))

Dew Point Condensation Alert

Compares air temperature against relative humidity to alert you when moisture will condense on telescope optics, allowing you to turn on dew heaters in advance.

8. 207-Object Catalog & Sky Chart

Complete starter database containing all 110 Messier objects, brightest NGC/IC emission nebulae, reflection nebulae, galaxies, planetary nebulae, open and globular clusters, and planets.

Every object includes peak transit times, magnitude, size in arcminutes, and a heuristic 0–100 imaging score.

  • Weather Sync: While connected at home, download hourly cloud cover and seeing forecasts from Open-Meteo.

10. FAQ & Troubleshooting

Q: Does AstroField require cell service in the field?
A: No. All astronomy ephemeris engines, celestial geometry, and catalog data run 100% on your device.

Q: How do I preserve my night vision?
A: Enable the Red Night Mode toggle in AstroField to apply a long-wavelength red filter across the entire app.
